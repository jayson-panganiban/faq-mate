### Definitions — Flood
**Clause Classification:** `Definition`

Under **AIA Home Loan Protection** (PDS) and the *Insurance Contracts Regulations 2012 (Cth)* Reg 29D standard definition, **Flood** means the covering of normally dry land by water that has escaped or been released from the normal confines of any lake, river, creek, or other natural watercourse (whether or not altered or modified), or any reservoir, canal, or dam.

**Related Water Peril Demarcation:**
- **Storm & Rainwater Run-off:** Surface water or rainwater run-off flowing over normally dry ground during a storm without escaping from a natural watercourse or dam is assessed under **Insured Event 3 (Storm cover)** rather than Flood.
- **Escape of Liquid:** Sudden bursting or leaking from fixed internal pipes, appliances, or hot water systems is assessed under **Insured Event 4 (Escape of Liquid)**.
- **Certificate of Insurance:** Your *Certificate of Insurance* (Policy Schedule) specifies whether Flood cover is included or opted out, along with any applicable Flood or Storm Excess.
- **Official Source URL:** [https://www.aia.com.au/content/dam/au-wise/en/docs/direct-life-ci/home-loan-protection/home-loan-protection-product-disclosure-statement.pdf](https://www.aia.com.au/content/dam/au-wise/en/docs/direct-life-ci/home-loan-protection/home-loan-protection-product-disclosure-statement.pdf)

#### CRITICAL POLICY EXCLUSIONS & LIMITATIONS (WHAT IS NOT COVERED):
- Actions of the Sea & Storm Surge: Loss or damage caused by high tide, king tides, tidal waves, or actions of the sea is excluded.
- 72-Hour Waiting Period (Embargo): No cover applies for flood, storm, bushfire, or cyclone during the first 72 hours of a new policy unless replacing equivalent cover without lapse.
- Retaining walls, sea walls, freestanding outdoor gates/fences, and gradual seepage/hydrostatic pressure are excluded.

> ⚠️ **Mandatory Embargo Window:** A strict **72-hour embargo** applies post-purchase. No cover for losses occurring within 72 hours of policy inception unless transferring without lapse.

When we pay The Accidental Death benefit pays a lump sum amount if you die within six months of an accidental injury, as a result of that accidental injury.

- occurs while you are participating in any professional sport

Duty of Disclosure Before you enter into or become insured under a contract of life insurance with an insurer, you have a duty of disclosure under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 to disclose to the insurer every matter that you know, or could reasonably be expected to know, that is relevant to the insurer’s decision whether to accept the risk of the insurance and, if so, on what terms.
You have the same duty to disclose those matters to the insurer before you extend, vary or reinstate a contract of insurance.
Your duty does not require disclosure of a matter:
- that diminishes the risk to be undertaken by the insurer
- that is of common knowledge
- that your insurer knows or, in the ordinary course of its business, ought to know, or
- as to which compliance with your duty is waived by the insurer.
Non-disclosure If you fail to comply with your duty of disclosure and the insurer would not have entered into the contract on any terms if the failure had not occurred, the insurer can avoid the contract within three years of entering into it. If your non-disclosure is fraudulent, the insurer may avoid the contract at any time.
An insurer who is entitled to avoid a contract of life insurance may within three years of entering into it, elect not to avoid it but reduce the sum that you have been insured for in accordance with a formula that takes account the premium that would have been payable if you had disclosed all the relevant matters to the insurer.
