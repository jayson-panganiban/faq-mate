## Section: Frequently asked questions - Motor

### How do I make a claim?

We’re here to make the claims process as simple and stress-free as possible. If you’re involved in a car crash, follow our helpful tips on what to do and the information to collect [after a crash](https://www.datocms-assets.com/49357/1758863546-crash-card-web-secure-pdf.pdf) and the steps below to get started.

**1. Avoid taking responsibility**

Do not admit fault, negotiate, or attempt to settle any claim without our consent.

If your vehicle is not safe to drive after the incident, do not use it until it has been inspected and repaired by a licensed repairer.

**2. Lodge your claim**

You can make a claim by:

- Calling us on 13 27 22, or
- In some cases, lodging a claim online

Each claim must relate to a single incident. If more than one incident has occurred, you’ll need to lodge a separate claim for each, and an excess may apply to each claim.

**3. What happens next**

Once your claim is lodged, we’ll guide you through the process. This may include:

Assessing the damage

Investigating the incident

Requesting additional information or documents

We’ll manage the claim for you, including handling, settling, or defending any covered claims made against you.

If you need help at any stage, our team is here to support you.

For full details about your cover, including what is and isn’t included, please refer to your [**Product Disclosure Statement (PDS)**](https://www.ract.com.au/insurance-and-finance/insurance-documents) and your **policy schedule**.

### How will my claim be settled?

If your claim is accepted, we will decide (acting reasonably and considering your preference) how to settle your claim. This may include:

- Repairing your car
- Declaring your car a total loss
- Paying a cash amount to cover repairs (subject to policy limits)

**Your level of cover matters:**

- **Comprehensive cover:** We will pay up to the agreed value shown on your policy schedule.
- **Third Party Property Damage or Third Party Fire & Theft:** The maximum payment *we* will make or be liable for is the *market value* of the car or $5,000, whichever is lesser.

We will deduct any applicable excesses and any unpaid premium (including any unpaid instalments for the period of insurance) from our payment.

### What is an excess and when do I have to pay it?

**What is an excess?**

An excess is the amount you contribute towards a claim. The excesses that apply to your policy are listed on your policy schedule.

**When do you have to pay an excess?**

You’ll need to pay any applicable excess for each claim. The amount will depend on:

- The type of incident
- Your policy terms
- Who was driving at the time
- The specific circumstances of the claim

**Types of excess**

Depending on your policy, one or more of the following may apply:

- **Basic excess**: Applies to all claims
- **Driver age excess (listed or unlisted)**: May apply based on the driver’s age and whether they are listed on your policy
- **Additional excess**: May apply for specific risks or conditions noted on your policy

**Paying your excess**

- You don’t need to pay your excess when you lodge your claim
- We’ll let you know how and when to pay
- The excess must be paid before your claim is finalized

**Important:** if youare experiencing [financial hardship](https://www.ract.com.au/insurance-and-finance/info-and-documents/vulnerability-financial-difficulty), please contact us to discuss how we may be able to assist. This could include deducting the excess from the amount to be paid under the claim or we could arrange a payment plan for you.

### What happens if my car is a total loss (see below for new cars)?

If your car is declared a total loss, what you receive depends on your level of cover:

**Comprehensive cover**

- We will pay up to the agreed value shown on your policy
- Any excesses and unpaid premiums will be deducted
- Your car, including any modifications and accessories, becomes our property

**Third Party Property Damage or Fire & Theft**

- We will pay the market value or $5,000 whichever is lesser
- Any excesses and unpaid premiums will be deducted
- your car including any modifications and accessories becomes our property, unless you ask to keep it and we agree, in which case we will be entitled to ask you to pay us its estimated salvage value.

 For all policies we will deduct any applicable excesses and any unpaid premium (including

any unpaid instalments for the period of insurance) from our payment.

If your car is being used as security for a loan, we may use part or all of your claim payment to first pay in full any sum owed to a financier. If we do this:

- we will pay any remaining balance to you; and
- our obligation to pay your claim under this policy is fully discharged.

### What happens if my new car is a total loss?

If you have **Comprehensive cover** and your new car is a total loss, we may replace it with a new one.

This applies if:

- You bought the car new (or as a demonstrator from a licensed dealer)
- It’s used for private purposes
- Less than two years have passed since the car was first registered
- The loss is an insured event covered by your policy

**What we’ll do:**

- Replace your car with a new one of the same make and model (if available in Australia)
- Include the same modifications or accessories
- Cover on-road costs

If the same model is no longer available, we’ll provide a similar new car with comparable features that is available in Australia.

If your car is less than two years old, is being used as security for a loan and is declared a total loss, we will need to receive written consent from your financier before we can replace your car.

If we replace your car, you must pay any applicable excesses and unpaid premium on your policy. The new car will continue to be covered under your policy for the remainder of the period of insurance.

If you do not want a new car, we will pay you the agreed value as shown on your policy schedule less any excesses and unpaid premium. In this case your policy will come to an end and no refund of premium will be due to you.

### What if I need a hire car?

This applies only if you have a Comprehensive policy.

**Hire car conditions**

We will arrange and pay for a hire car through one of our providers.

You are responsible for all running costs and any extra expenses of the hire car,

including:

- any deposit required by the hire car company; and
- any additional days outside of the authorised hire period.

**Hire car after theft**

Subject to the Hire car conditions, if you have Comprehensive cover and your car is stolen, we’ll provide a small category hire car for up to 14 days.

**Hire car after a not-at-fault accident**

If you have Comprehensive cover, we’ll provide a small category hire car for up to 14 days if:

- You’re involved in an accident caused entirely by another driver
- Your claim is covered
- You can provide details of the at-fault driver (e.g. name, address, registration)
- Your car can’t be safely driven or is being repaired

**Optional 30 day hire car**

This optional cover is an additional cost and if selected will be shown on your policy schedule.

If you have this option we will provide you with a small category hire car and pay the rental fee for up to 30 days where your claim is covered by the policy and your car cannot be safely driven or is in for repairs.

This option applies in addition to any other hire car you may be entitled to.

It can apply to other types of claims, not just theft or not-at-fault accidents

**When the hire car must be returned**

You’ll need to return the hire car as soon as reasonably possible when:

- Your car is repaired or recovered
- The maximum hire period is reached
- You receive a cash settlement
- Your car is declared a total loss and paid
- You receive a replacement car

### What do I need to know about cash settlements?

A cash settlement is when we pay you money instead of arranging repairs.

**How much will be paid?**

- **If you request a cash settlement:**

We’ll pay what it would have cost us to repair your car (within policy limits)

- **If we offer a cash settlement:**

We’ll pay a reasonable cost to repair your car (within policy limits)

- Any applicable **excesses and unpaid premiums** will be deducted from the amount

**Why might a cash settlement be offered?**

Common reasons include:

- You don’t want your car repaired
- You want to use a repairer we can’t directly authorise

**Other things to know**

If your car’s condition before the incident prevents us from repairing it, we may:

- Ask you to contribute to repair costs, or
- Offer a cash settlement instead

**Important things to know**

- If your car is financed, we may use part or all of your claim payment to pay the lender first. If this happens, any remaining amount will be paid to you.
- If you receive a cash settlement, you’re responsible for arranging and paying for your own repairs. We don’t guarantee the quality, cost, or timing of these repairs.

You must keep your car in a safe and roadworthy condition at all times. This includes:

- Fixing any damage that could make the car unsafe
- Letting us know about any existing damage when your policy starts
- Delays in repairs may lead to further damage. Any additional costs may be your responsibility.
- After major events (like natural disasters), repair costs may increase and repair times may be longer due to high demand.

If you have any questions about your policy or the claims process, please contact us — we’re here to help.

### What is a scope of works?

**What is a scope of works for a home building insurance claim?**

The scope of works is a document that plays an important role in us deciding what damage to your property is covered by your insurance policy.

The scope of works is a list of home building repair works for your insurance claim. It can help identify and outline what repair or rebuilding work is needed to fix things. It is used to obtain repair quotes.

**Why is a scope of works needed?**

We may need to prepare a scope of works when your home building insurance claim is for a higher value, involves major damage, or is otherwise complex to fix because of the need to coordinate multiple trades or contractors.

**How do you prepare a scope of works?**

Our loss adjuster usually assists with co-ordinating the preparation of the scope of works undertaken by builders and where relevant, with the engagement of engineers/designers. Their assessment to inform the scope of work may be done virtually or by visiting your property to inspect the damage and ask for your information.

**What should I expect with the process?**

The process for developing a scope of works may vary depending on your insurer and your unique individual circumstances.

Developing a scope of works can involve several discussions, and even the development of several versions before it is complete. This is to make sure it accurately reflects the extent of work required to complete repairs or rebuild your property.

As you best know your property, you can point out subtle damage caused by the event that our loss adjustor (or other person preparing the scope of works) may not otherwise notice when they visit your property.

The initial scope of works may need to be reassessed once repairs have commenced, if new information becomes available about the damage that needs to be fixed.

There may be circumstances when specific repairs are not included in a scope of works. This may be because there is damage to your property that is not covered by your insurance policy.

It is important you take the time to properly understand the scope of works.

**How do I find out more?**

Feel free to ask us, or our representatives any questions to clarify your understanding. You can also seek independent advice from a qualified builder or tradesperson, or other expert (e.g. a building consultant) about the scope of works.

**How do I lodge a complaint about the scope of works?**

We have a complaints process. You can contact us if you have a complaint about the scope of works at any time. More information about making a complaint is on our [website](https://www.datocms-assets.com/49357/1783483789-ract11090-complaints-handing-guide-update-web-op3.pdf), including how to access the Australian Financial Complaints Authority if you are unhappy with our response.
