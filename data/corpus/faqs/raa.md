### What is the arrangement between RAA and Allianz?

We’ve joined forces with global insurance firm Allianz’s Australian business under a 20-year partnership that will see them underwrite RAA insurance policies under the trusted RAA brand.

Of course, we're still local – whether you’re making a claim or taking out an insurance policy – you’ll still be speaking to a South Australian.

At RAA, being a small player on a global stage and having all our risk in South Australia meant it was getting harder for us to deliver the value and benefits you’ve come to trust when you insure with RAA.

The partnership will help strengthen RAA’s future, enabling us to reduce our risk exposure and deliver more value for members. Importantly, RAA will remain a member-based organisation.

### What is an excess?

If you make a claim, you may need to pay us an amount towards the claim. The amount you pay us is an excess. 

There are different types of excesses which may apply to your claim including basic excess and other additional excesses. Any excesses that apply to you are shown when you get a quote and on your Certificate of Insurance. 

There are some incidents where you won’t need to pay an excess like if the driver of your car didn’t cause the damage and you’ve provided us the necessary details including the information of the person who caused the damage. 

See the relevant [Product Disclosure Statement](https://www.raa.com.au/help-centre/insurance-support/pds-and-fact-sheets) for full details.

### Do I have to pay an excess if an accident wasn't my fault?

No, you won't need to pay an excess if we decide, acting reasonably, you were not 'at fault' for an accident.

If you're not 'at fault' it means you could provide us with the details of who caused the accident, and after an investigation we decide that person's actions were the main cause, and not yours. 

When no one is at fault like in a hail storm or if a tree falls on your car, or if you can't identify who caused the accident (e.g. if they hit your car in a carpark but didn't stop or leave a note) you'll still need to pay us any excesses that apply to you. 

Check the [Product Disclosure Statement](https://www.raa.com.au/help-centre/insurance-support/pds-and-fact-sheets) for more information.

### Do I need insurance?

There are different types of insurance cover, so make sure to assess what’s right for you. Insurance can protect you from financial loss if, for example, your car or home and/or its contents are damaged, destroyed or stolen. ## Section: Cover

### What's the difference between covers?

The three most common types of insurance are third party, third party fire & theft, and comprehensive.

**Third Party**
Covers damage caused by your car to someone else's vehicle or property.

**Third Party, Fire & Theft**
Covers damage caused by your car to someone else's vehicle or property, as well as cover for if your car is damaged by fire or stolen.

**Comprehensive**
Covers damage to your car, as well as damage caused by your car to someone else's vehicle or property, and cover if your car is stolen or damaged by fire.

We offer two levels of comprehensive insurance. Check the [comparison table](https://www.raa.com.au/motor/car-insurance) to see the benefits for each type of cover that we offer.

### What's the difference between CTP and Third Party insurance?

Compulsory Third Party (CTP) covers your costs for the injuries of any other person hurt in an accident that involves your car. RAA doesn't offer CTP. It's compulsory to choose a CTP insurer when your pay your car registration and is entirely separate to Third Party insurance.

Third Party insurance covers damage by your car to someone else's car or property. RAA does offer Third Party insurance. You may want to have RAA Car Insurance in addition to CTP.

### What's the difference between our Premium and Standard Comprehensive cover?

Both levels of Comprehensive insurance cover damage to your car, as well as damage caused by your car to someone else's vehicle or property, and cover if your car is stolen or damaged by fire.

**Premium Comprehensive** is our car insurance with the highest level of cover. It has four benefits you can't get with Standard:

- **Unlimited $0 excess windscreen repair or replacement**
Repair or replacement of your windscreen, window or roof glass. You won't pay an excess for windscreen or window glass breakage claims.

- **$200 mobility benefit**
When your claim is settled, we'll make a $200 payment to you to help with transport costs you might have had after select incidents.

- **Tools of trade**
We'll cover up to $3,000 for your tools of trade if they're accidentally damaged or stolen from your car.

- **5 year new vehicle replacement**
If your brand new car is written off within 5 years of registration, we'll replace it with the same model or a comparable one if unavailable. If we can’t replace it within 90 days, we’ll offer a cash settlement.

**Standard Comprehensive** doesn't have the above four benefits, but it does have:

- **2 year new vehicle replacement**If your brand new car is written off within 2 years of registration, we'll replace it with the same model or a comparable one if unavailable. If we can’t replace it within 90 days, we’ll offer a cash settlement.

- **Optional cover: One $0 excess windscreen repair or replacement**You can choose to add one $0 excess repair or replacement of your windscreen, window or roof glass to your Standard Comprehensive cover. You may have to pay the basic excess if you make more than one windscreen claim in the same term of your policy.

### Are any of my contents covered if I take them out of my home?

Our contents insurance includes cover for your contents while they’re temporarily removed from your home to another residential address or storage facility. This cover is capped at 10% of the sum insured.

### What's an excess?

If you make a claim, you may need to pay us an amount towards the claim. The amount you pay us is an excess.

There are different types of excesses which may apply to your claim including basic excess and other additional excesses. These are shown on your Certificate of Insurance documents.

There are some incidents where you won’t need to pay an excess, like if the driver of your car didn’t cause the damage and you’ve provided us the necessary details including the information of the person who caused the damage.

In the case of repairs to your car, any applicable excess needs to be paid to the repairer upon collection of your vehicle once repairs are completed. In the case of a total loss, the excess will be deducted from the amount we pay you under the claim.

For more information on excesses, refer to the relevant [Product Disclosure Statement](https://www.raa.com.au/help-centre/insurance-support/pds-and-fact-sheets) for your policy.

## Section: Claims – Common questions

### Will your premium go up on renewal because you made a claim?

If you were determined to be Not at Fault in the Incident,
that is, you did not have to pay your Excess, or if your claim
was not of a kind that required you to pay your Excess, then
your premium will not be affected by this claim.

If you were At Fault in the Incident (that is, you did have to
pay your Excess):

- your base premium may be affected ([refer to ‘Premium factors’ on pages 9-10](#about-the-premium));
- if you currently hold a rating 1, your rating 1 discount will
not be affected by this claim; or
- if you do not currently hold a rating 1, your premium will
be affected by this claim because your ‘insurance rating’
will be less. For example, a rating 2 would fall to a rating
3 on renewal ([refer to ‘insurance rating’ on page 11](#insurance-rating-discount)).

### Will we cancel your Policy if you make too many claims?

We may decline to renew your Policy depending on the number and type of claims you make. On renewal of your Policy, we may apply a Non-removable Excess. We will not cancel your Policy during the term, unless your Vehicle is a Total Loss or one of the circumstances set out on [page 42](#claims-repair-or-cash) applies.
