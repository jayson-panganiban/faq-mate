### Excesses
**Clause Classification:** `SubLimit`

An excess is the amount which you may be required to pay if you make a claim.
The excesses which you have to pay are set out in this Policy Wording or on your Policy Schedule.
If a claim or series of claims arise from one occurrence causing loss or damage to both your home and contents you’ll only have to pay the highest of the total excess(es) applicable to each.
If a claim or series of claims arise from one occurrence causing loss or damage under both Section 1 - Home and Section 5 - Farm Property, you’ll only have to pay the highest of the total excess(es) applicable to each section.
How your excess is applied to items with a maximum limit Where a maximum limit is applicable under ‘Maximum limits we will pay’, the excess will be applied to the claim prior to applying the maximum limit. For example, if a ring worth $12,000 was stolen from the home and it has not been specified, the $1,000 excess is deducted from the $12,000 ring value and the $10,000 per jewellery maximum limit would apply. Therefore, $10,000 would be payable.
If the ring had been specified for $12,000, we would pay $11,000, being the $12,000 ring value less the $1,000 excess.
If the stolen ring was worth only $2,000, we would pay $1,000, being the $2,000 ring value less the $1,000 excess.
When you will not have to pay an excess You will not have to pay an excess if we agree to pay a claim as a result of damage that renders your home, contents or both a total loss.
