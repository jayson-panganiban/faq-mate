### What do I do if I’ve missed a monthly payment?

If you pay your premiums monthly and you’ve missed a payment, you may have received a text, email or letter from us asking you to [make a payment](https://www.einsure.com.au/redirect/allianz-com-au-online-payment). The easiest way to make a payment is online using your Biller Reference Number (BRN).

If you don’t make a payment, we’ll try to take a double payment on the next instalment date. It’s important to make sure you have enough funds on your next instalment date. If we can’t make two direct debits in a row, we may cancel your policy. Don’t worry, we’ll let you know if the payment fails a second time and before cancelling your policy.

If your payment fails twice, you can still make a manual payment online or by calling our team as long as the policy is still active.

You may want to also check and update your payment details.

### Why is my insurance premium higher than last year?

Your premium is based on your circumstances, the cover you’ve chosen and your likelihood of making a claim. When you update or renew your insurance policy, your premium may change. For example, changing your sum insured amount, excess level or optional covers can change your premium.

Other factors outside of your personal circumstances can also affect your premium. Things like increases in claims costs and inflation or the likelihood of future weather events can mean an increase in your renewal premium.

Learn more about [how your premium is calculated.](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/resources/insights/understanding-insurance-premiums)

### Do I need to list every driver on my policy?

No, we’ve made things simple. You only need to list the main driver. The main driver is the person who uses the car the most. We cover anyone who drives the car as long as they have the main driver’s permission. There’s an Under 25 excess you’ll need to pay if someone under the age of 25 drives the car, gets into an accident and makes a claim. However, if you like, you can reduce this excess amount by listing the under 25 driver on your policy. Keep in mind that this information is for customers who buy a new Car Insurance policy. Our policies have changed over the years, so your cover may differ based on when you first purchased it. Visit our policy page for more information.

### What is an excess and how does it affect my premium?

An excess is an amount that you must pay towards each claim you make. An example: Imagine your car is damaged in a covered accident and needs $7,000 of repairs. If your policy has a $1,100 excess, then you’ll need to pay the $1,100 excess and your car insurance will cover the remaining $5,900 for the cost of repairs. Generally, choosing a higher excess can lower your premium, while choosing a lower excess will increase it. With Allianz, there are two excesses. Basic excess: A basic excess is the standard amount you must pay out of pocket when making a claim on your car insurance policy. Under 25 Driver excess: An additional excess may apply if the driver is under 25 or inexperienced. There may be additional excesses depending on your circumstances.

### Will I need a cover note or insurance certificate before I can drive?

Before you get behind the wheel, you must have Compulsory Third Party (CTP) insurance. This is the legal minimum in every state and territory in Australia. CTP covers injury to other people if you’re in an accident. It doesn’t cover you or other people’s cars. Other types of car insurance, like Third Party Property or Comprehensive cover, are optional. These cover damage to other vehicles, property, or your own car, but you’re not legally required to have them to drive. We’ll send you a confirmation email with your insurance certificate as soon as you buy an Allianz Car Insurance policy. You can set the start date for up to 45 days from when you get your quote.

## Section: Home Insurance

### How much should I insure my home for?

You’ll need enough [cover to rebuild your home](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/articles/how-much-to-insure-house-for.html) and replace what you own if it’s lost or damaged. This is called your ‘sum insured’. It’s based on rebuild costs, not your property’s sale price.

If your cover is too low, you may need to pay the difference yourself, so it’s worth reviewing it regularly. If your cover is too high, we won’t pay more than your actual loss. We‘ll pay either the cost of your loss or your policy limit, whichever is less.

### I just bought a house, what insurance should I consider?

Let’s break it down by house type.

**Freestanding house**

You may want to consider:

- [Building Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/building.html) – covers the physical structure (walls, roof, garage, fences, etc.)
- [Contents Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/contents.html) – covers the things you own inside the home
- Or [Home & Contents Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/home-and-contents.html) – combines both on one policy

Why: You’re responsible for the entire property, the building and everything inside it.

**Townhouse or villa**

You may want to consider:

- [Building Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/building.html) – if you own the building and it’s not covered by a body corporate/strata (check this with your strata)
- [Contents Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/contents.html)
- Check your strata/body corporate plan – sometimes they insure shared structures but not the interior of your home – this includes any fixtures, fittings or structural improvements that you may be legally responsible for.

Why: Ownership varies. Some complexes include building cover, some don’t.

**Apartment (strata‑titled)**

You may want to consider:

- [Contents Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/contents.html) only

Why: The building itself is usually covered by strata insurance, but that doesn’t include your personal belongings, flooring, internal fixtures (you’re legally responsible for), or valuables inside your unit.

**Investment property (you’re the landlord)**

You may want to consider:

- [Landlord Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/landlord.html) – you have the option to take out building only or contents only cover, or both, depending on your situation.

Why: Being a landlord brings different risks, like vandalism or malicious damage or loss of rent after an insured event6

**If you're renting (tenant)**

You may want to consider:

- [Renter Insurance](https://www.allianz.com.au/content/onemarketing/aal/au_site/en_AU/home-insurance/renters.html) – Allianz provides Renter Insurance (this is different from our Contents Insurance cover).

Why: The building belongs to someone else, but you may need cover for your contents.

## Section: Policy Information

### What is a Product Disclosure Statement?

A Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) explains what you are and aren’t covered for, what are insured events, any applicable claim limits, all policy exclusions, and the claims process. The PDS is designed to help you decide if an insurance product is what you need. There may also be a Supplementary PDS (SPDS) included with the PDS which updates the PDS. For example, an SPDS may be provided that replaces the Duty of Disclosure with the Duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation.

*Canonical Source:* [https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html](https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html)

---

### What is a key facts sheet?

A key facts sheet provides a summary of some of what is and isn’t covered by a Home or Landlord policy. It also sets out some of the events that are and aren’t covered. For full details of the conditions, limits, and exclusions on all our cover options always refer to the PDS.

*Canonical Source:* [https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html](https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html)

---

### What is a Premium, Excess, Discount and Options Guide?

A Premium, Excess, Discount and Options Guide (PED) provides information about how premiums are calculated, what discounts can be applied to your premiums and how they’re applied, when certain excesses need to be paid following certain claims and the options available under your policy. A PED is available for Car Insurance.

*Canonical Source:* [https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html](https://www.allianz.com.au/my-allianz/policy-information.html)

---

### What is a Target Market Determination?

A Target Market Determination (TMD) describes the class of customers for which the products described in a PDS have been designed for, including the key attributes of those products and the customers who are eligible for it. A TMD doesn’t replace the terms and conditions, or the disclosures made, in a PDS. Always refer to the PDS before deciding to purchase an insurance cover.
